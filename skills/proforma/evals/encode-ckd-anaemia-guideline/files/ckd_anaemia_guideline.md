# Anaemia in Chronic Kidney Disease — Practice Recommendations (FICTIONAL TEST DOCUMENT)

*This is a synthetic guideline written for software testing. It is not clinical advice.*

Levels of evidence: I (RCTs/meta-analyses) to V (expert opinion). Grades of recommendation: A (strongly recommended) to E (never recommended).

## Algorithm (Figure 1)

1. **Screening.** Measure haemoglobin (Hb, g/dL) at least annually in CKD stage 3, and every 3 months in stages 4–5 [II, B].
2. If Hb < 13 g/dL in men or < 12 g/dL in women, the patient has anaemia → **Diagnostic work-up**; otherwise continue routine screening.
3. **Diagnostic work-up** (performed in parallel): full blood count with red cell indices; iron studies (ferritin, transferrin saturation TSAT); vitamin B12 and folate; exclusion of bleeding.
4. **Correct iron deficiency first**, then consider erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESA).
5. **Re-check Hb** 4 weeks after any treatment change; the cycle continues while the patient remains under nephrology follow-up.

## Recommendations

### Iron
- Give iron when TSAT ≤ 30% and ferritin ≤ 500 ng/mL [I, A].
- Oral iron is preferred in non-dialysis patients [II, B]; intravenous iron is preferred in patients on haemodialysis [I, A].
- Iron is not recommended in patients with active systemic infection [III, C].

### Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents
- Consider an ESA when Hb < 10 g/dL after iron deficiency has been corrected [I, A].
- ESA should not be started in patients with active malignancy or a history of stroke [II, D].
- Target Hb 10–11.5 g/dL; do not intentionally exceed 13 g/dL [I, A].
- In patients aged 75 or older, the panel did not reach consensus on ESA use: some members would consider it after shared decision-making [III, C], others would not [III, D].

### Transfusion
- Red cell transfusion is reserved for Hb < 7 g/dL or symptomatic anaemia with haemodynamic instability [II, B].
